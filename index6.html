Final Sınavı — Tam Metin (Grup A)

Eastern Mediterranean University — PSIR 101 Introduction to Global Politics — Final Examination, 16 January 2025 — 120 minutes — 40 points

Instructors: Prof. Dr. Erol Kaymak, Assoc. Prof. Dr. Umut Bozkurt

Section A: Multiple Choice (30 × 1 pt = 30 pts)

1. Which concept describes the principle that states should bear responsibility based on their historical contribution to climate change?
a) Tragedy of the Commons   b) Common But Differentiated Responsibilities   c) Collective security   d) Free-rider principle
2. The Responsibility to Protect (R2P) was formally endorsed at:
a) Bretton Woods   b) 2005 UN World Summit   c) Treaty of Westphalia   d) Rio Earth Summit
3. Which generation of human rights focuses on economic, social, and cultural rights?
a) First   b) Second   c) Third   d) Fourth
4. The concept of "three generations of rights" was developed by:
a) Rawls   b) Morgenthau   c) Karel Vasak   d) Kant
5. The Stockholm Conference (1972) was significant because it:
a) Created the WTO   b) Ended the Cold War   c) Was the first major international environmental conference   d) Established NATO
6. The Paris Agreement primarily addresses:
a) Nuclear proliferation   b) Trade liberalisation   c) Climate change mitigation   d) Maritime disputes
7. Which of the following best describes sustainability?
a) Economic growth at all costs   b) Meeting present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs   c) Military self-sufficiency   d) Technological innovation
8. The IPCC is:
a) An international court   b) A military alliance   c) The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change   d) A trade organisation
9. Which concept describes when a state's sovereignty is conditional on protecting its population?
a) Absolute sovereignty   b) Sovereignty as responsibility   c) Negative sovereignty   d) Popular sovereignty
10. The "Tragedy of the Commons" was popularised by:
a) Marx   b) Hardin   c) Garrett Hardin   d) Ostrom
11. Post-colonial critiques of human rights argue that:
a) Rights are unnecessary   b) Universal frameworks can reflect Western cultural assumptions   c) Only economic rights matter   d) Rights should be abolished
12. Which case is most commonly cited as a failure of international intervention?
a) Kosovo 1999   b) Libya 2011   c) Rwanda 1994   d) East Timor 1999
13. East Mediterranean energy coordination primarily involves disputes over:
a) Nuclear energy   b) Solar energy   c) Offshore natural gas reserves   d) Wind farms
14. Intersectionality in human rights refers to:
a) Treaty overlap   b) How race, gender, class interact to create unique oppression   c) Economic interdependence   d) Military alliances
15. The UN Security Council's veto power is held by:
a) All members   b) Five permanent members   c) Secretary General   d) General Assembly
16. UNFICYP refers to:
a) Trade agreement   b) UN peacekeeping force in Cyprus   c) EU institution   d) NATO operation
17. The free-rider problem in environmental politics describes:
a) States competing   b) States benefiting without contributing   c) States imposing tariffs   d) Courts penalising polluters
18. The Annan Plan for Cyprus proposed:
a) Full unification   b) A bi-zonal, bi-communal federation   c) Permanent partition   d) Continued UN administration
19. Turkey's policy regarding Cyprus since 2004 has increasingly:
a) Supported rapid reunification   b) Withdrawn military forces   c) Favoured a two-state solution   d) Joined EU accession talks
20. Which feminist IR concept argues security should include everyday violence?
a) Balance of power   b) Collective security   c) Human security   d) State security
21. East Mediterranean gas coordination refers to:
a) Nuclear cooperation   b) Offshore gas exploration disputes   c) Solar panel trade   d) Wind energy treaties
22. Which best describes sustainability in IR?
a) Military power   b) Balancing present and future needs   c) Trade balance   d) Diplomatic immunity
23. The two-state discourse in Cyprus refers to:
a) Reunification   b) Recognition of two separate sovereign states   c) EU membership   d) NATO membership
24. R2P's three pillars include:
a) Sanctions only   b) State responsibility, international assistance, decisive response   c) Military force only   d) Economic development
25. The CBDR principle is most relevant to:
a) Security studies   b) Trade law   c) Climate change negotiations   d) Human rights law
26. Which UN body authorises the use of force?
a) General Assembly   b) Security Council   c) ECOSOC   d) ICJ
27. The role of UNEP is to:
a) Adjudicate disputes   b) Manage trade   c) Coordinate environmental activities   d) Deploy peacekeepers
28. Humanitarian intervention is most contentious because it:
a) Is too expensive   b) Takes too long   c) Challenges state sovereignty   d) Requires unanimous vote
29. The concept of "environmental justice" primarily addresses:
a) Military pollution   b) Trade in hazardous goods   c) Diplomatic immunity   d) Disproportionate environmental burdens on marginalised communities
30. The Kyoto Protocol differed from the Paris Agreement in that:
a) It had no targets   b) It applied only to developing countries   c) It set binding targets primarily for developed countries   d) It focused on biodiversity

Section B: Essay Questions (choose 2 of 4, 5 pts each = 10 pts)

1. Trace the development of human rights since WWII. Discuss the three generations, intersections with race and gender, and post-colonial critiques.
2. Analyse the tension between state sovereignty and international intervention. Discuss R2P, Security Council authorisation, and case studies.
3. Evaluate the evolution of international environmental politics from Stockholm to Paris. Discuss Tragedy of the Commons, CBDR, and the free-rider problem.
4. Analyse the Cyprus conflict with reference to historical division, the Annan Plan, UNFICYP, Turkey's evolving policy, and East Mediterranean energy politics.

Vize Sınavı — Tam Metin ve Cevap Anahtarı

INTL 511 — Midterm Examination, Fall 2024-25 — 120 minutes — 100 points

Section A: Concept Identification (7 × 5 pts = 35 pts) — Define and explain the significance for IR theory.

1. Structural Realism vs. Classical Realism
2. Relative Gains vs. Absolute Gains
3. Explanatory Theory vs. Constitutive Theory
4. Cox's Problem-Solving Theory vs. Critical Theory
5. Complex Interdependence
6. Marxist Hegemony in IR
7. English School: Sovereignty and R2P Tension

Section B: Analytical Comparison (1 × 35 pts) — Choose ONE:

a) Critically evaluate neoliberal institutionalism as a response to structural realism.
b) How does critical theory challenge the legitimacy of existing international institutions?

Section C: Reflective Assessment (1 × 30 pts)

Reflect on how your understanding of IR theory has evolved during the first half of this course.

Cevap Anahtarı — Bölüm A

1. Waltz'un sistem düzeyi (anarşi, güç dağılımı) vs. Morgenthau'nun insan doğası odağı. Birim düzeyi değişkenler paranteze alınır vs. devlet adamlarının algıları.

2. Realistlerde diğer tarafın kazancı önemli (göreli); liberallerde toplam kazanç önemli (mutlak). İşbirliği imkânları doğrudan etkilenir.

3. Pozitivist nedensellik vs. normlar ve söylem tarafından inşa. Wendt, Onuf kilit figürler.

4. Mevcut yapıyı kabul et ve optimize et vs. yapının kendisini sorgula. "Theory is always for someone and for some purpose."

5. Keohane ve Nye: çoklu kanallar, askeri gücün azalan önceliği, gündem hiyerarşisi yokluğu.

6. Gramsci'nin rıza ve zor birleşimi; Cox'un uluslararası uyarlaması. Maddi kapasite, kurumlar, fikirler üçlüsü.

7. Pluralist vs. solidarist tartışma; Bull'un anarşik toplum kavramı; egemenlik normu ile R2P gerilimi.

Bölüm B ve C

Birincil kaynak kullanımı, karşıt argüman değerlendirmesi, teorik tutarlılık, kişisel akademik gelişim beklenir.

Final Sınavı — Tam Metin ve Cevap Anahtarı

PSIR 308 — Final Examination, Spring 2024-25 — 120 minutes — 40 points

Section A: Concept Identification (7 × 3 pts = 21 pts)

1. Derrida's Deconstruction and Différance
2. Said's Orientalism and Representation
3. Butler's Performative Gender and Precarity
4. Žižek's Ideology Critique and the Post-Political
5. Mouffe's Agonistic Democracy
6. Agamben's State of Exception and Bare Life
7. Sen's Capability Approach and Development as Freedom

Section B: Integrative Essay (1 × 19 pts) — Choose ONE:

a) How do post-structuralist thinkers (Derrida, Foucault, Butler) challenge Enlightenment assumptions about rationality and progress?
b) Compare Mouffe's agonistic democracy with Habermas's deliberative democracy. Which better addresses contemporary political polarisation?
c) Evaluate the contributions of Said, Butler, and Sen to expanding the boundaries of political thought beyond Western liberal traditions.

Cevap Anahtarı — Bölüm A

1. Derrida: Yapısöküm, différance, metafizik eleştirisi, konukseverlik, gelen demokrasi (democracy to come).

2. Said: Oryantalizm, temsil politikaları, bilgi/iktidar, post-kolonyal eleştiri, entelektüelin rolü.

3. Butler: Performatif toplumsal cinsiyet, yas tutulabilirlik, şiddet etiği, prekarite, normatif çerçevelerin sorgulanması.

4. Žižek: İdeoloji eleştirisi, Lacan ve siyaset, büyük Öteki, kapitalizm analizi, post-politik durum.

5. Mouffe: Agonistik demokrasi, siyasal olan, düşman vs. rakip (antagonism vs. agonism), sol popülizm, liberal demokrasi eleştirisi.

6. Agamben: İstisna hali, çıplak yaşam (homo sacer), biyopolitika, kamplar ve modern siyaset, egemenlik.

7. Sen: Yapabilirlik yaklaşımı, özgürlük olarak kalkınma, kamusal akıl, açlık ve demokrasi, karşılaştırmalı adalet.
